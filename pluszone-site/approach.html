CORE FRAMEWORK I

From Higher Rapport to solidifying values: the four processes

PROCESS 1Higher Rapport

A state of trust, one step beyond rapport, built on educational love. It forms when we go beyond understanding and empathy to ask “How can I help?”, and grows into a relationship where teacher and student grow together.

PROCESS 2Diagnosing with Questions

Not with blame or assumptions, but with questions and listening, we accurately diagnose the inner thoughts and feelings behind every behavior. Diagnostic skill is a teacher’s expertise.

PROCESS 3Setting Value Standards

Rather than one-way instruction or lectures, the Three-Step Questioning Method helps students realize and establish the right value standards for themselves.

PROCESS 4Solidifying Value Standards

Through consistent follow-up, we warmly encourage and support students so their value standards become habits in school life.

Higher Rapport, which begins with the teacher’s reflection, meets the Three-Step Questioning Method to set right value standards within students. The final destination of the four processes is for the ten universal values to become habits in students’ lives.
CORE FRAMEWORK II

Instead of lecturing… the Three-Step Questioning Method

Step 1 · Value RecognitionSeeing your behavior objectively
Step 2 · Clarifying ValuesRealizing why right values matter
Step 3 · Applying ValuesA voluntary commitment to act

1Step 1: Value Recognition

PurposeHelp students look at their behavior or the current problem objectively, without feeling resentful.

  • “Was there a reason you said that?”
  • “Was there a reason you did that?”
  • “Why do you think that happened?”
  • “Why did you feel that way?”
Teacher’s mindset tip

Don’t point out faults or question like an interrogation. Ask with a genuine wish to help, so the student can answer honestly without shrinking back.

2Step 2: Clarifying Value StandardsLet them judge → Ask back → Sum up the value

PurposeHelp students find for themselves why right value standards matter and how their behavior affects others.

  • “Is that right, or not right?”
  • “If you say that, will your friends trust you or not?”
  • “If you act that way, will your friends like it or dislike it?”
  • “If you speak or act that way, will anyone like you for it?”
Teacher’s mindset tip

Don’t rush if the student hesitates or doesn’t give the expected answer. Ask again with follow-up questions and wait until they find the answer themselves.

3Step 3: Applying Values

PurposeThe student chooses for themselves how to apply the right value in daily life.

  • “So what would be good to do from now on?”
  • “What could you practice to do that?”
  • “Then what do you need to do?”
Teacher’s mindset tip

Instead of explaining and deciding for them, ask so the student chooses. Truly respect the child’s choice, and warmly encourage even small commitments.

THE TEN VALUES

Ten universal values schools should emphasize

Values that human conscience recognizes as good regardless of race, religion or nationality: the common ground found in the two authors’ 34 and 22 years of teaching.

01Honesty

Not lying and not deceiving

02Sincerity

Steadiness: putting your heart into something and practicing it again and again

03Diligence

Doing whatever comes your way without putting it off

04Consideration

A heart and actions that put others at ease

05Understanding

Accepting others’ differences and circumstances with an open heart

06Forgiveness

No longer hating or resenting others for their wrongs

07Love

A heart and actions that help and care for others

08Positivity

The mindset that says “I can do it!”

09Proactivity

The decision to say “I will,” and the ability to act on it

10Truthfulness

A sincere heart without falsehood that embraces all the values above
