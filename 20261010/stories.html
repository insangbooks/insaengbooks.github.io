CLASSROOM CASES

How questions and empathy changed classrooms

Honesty chosen with a red pencil

Case 1 · Jinsu, 4th grade

The situation
After the final exam, Jinsu brought an answer sheet claiming it had been marked wrong, but the answer, originally 1, had been changed to 4.
What the teacher did
The teacher talked with Jinsu quietly after the other students left. When Jinsu stayed silent, the teacher didn’t press, handed over a red pencil so Jinsu could mark the answer, and waited.
What changed
Jinsu crossed out the answer, choosing honesty, and left with a proud, bright face. It never happened again.
The conversation

Teacher:Jinsu, mark it yourself. A circle if it’s right, a cross if it’s wrong. A score you get by cheating isn’t your real ability. I hope you make the brave choice that lets you stand tall.

(A moment later, Jinsu brings back the answer sheet with a cross on it.)

Teacher:Thank you for choosing honesty. Being honest makes you feel proud and at ease, doesn’t it?

Student:(smiling brightly) Yes.

A lie is a lie, even as a joke

Case 2 · Cheolsu, after a fight with a friend

The situation
Cheolsu told a lie as a joke. When Youngsu called Cheolsu a “liar,” a fight broke out, and Cheolsu blamed Youngsu.
What the teacher did
Instead of lecturing, the teacher used the Three-Step Questioning Method, following up with “Why isn’t it right?” and “Who would it hurt?”
What changed
Cheolsu realized that lying harms not only friends but also oneself, and decided what to do from now on.
The conversation

Teacher:Cheolsu, is lying right, or not right?

Student:It’s not right.

Teacher:Why isn’t it right? Who would it hurt?

Student:Youngsu… and it’s not good for me either. It can start a fight.

Teacher:So what would be good to do from now on?

Student:I shouldn’t lie, even as a joke.

The real reason, heard by asking instead of scolding

Case 3 · Beomsu, 6th grade

The situation
Beomsu kept bringing a toy puppy and playing with it in class, even after being told for over a month not to. Asked why, Beomsu sat silent, head down, for 20 minutes.
What the teacher did
The teacher reflected on having asked like an interrogation, and made it clear the question came from real curiosity, not from wanting to scold.
What changed
Beomsu finally shared what was inside, promised to keep the toy in the bag during class, and brightened up.
The conversation

Teacher:Beomsu, you usually don’t bring things you’re told not to, but you keep bringing this one. I’m not trying to scold you. I’m really curious.

Student:This toy is like my little brother.

Teacher:Do you feel anxious without it?

Student:Yes.

Teacher:Then how about keeping it in your bag and not taking it out during class?

Student:(face brightening) Okay!
